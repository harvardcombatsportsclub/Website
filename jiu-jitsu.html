Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu

The gentle art: control, leverage, and submissions, with no striking. Three practices a week at the Quad Recreation Center, open to every Harvard student regardless of experience.

When & where

    Quad Recreation Center (QRAC), multipurpose room. Full schedule →

    What a practice looks like

    Practices open with a dynamic warm-up and movement drills, move into technique instruction that you drill with a partner, and finish with positional sparring or live rolling for those who want it. Rolling is always optional for beginners.

    Some sessions are gi (the traditional kimono, with grips on the jacket and pants) and some are no-gi (shorts and a rash guard, faster pace). Check the schedule; if you don't own a gi yet, tell an officer and we'll sort you out.

    Before your first class

    What to bring & how we roll

    Wear

    Athletic clothing with no zippers, pockets, or buckles. Shorts or leggings and a fitted shirt are perfect for no-gi. Bring a water bottle. Mouthguards are optional but recommended once you start rolling.

    Hygiene

    Trim your nails, wear clean clothes to every session, and stay home if you have any skin issue or feel sick. Mat hygiene is how we keep everyone training.

    Mat rules

    Control your movements, communicate with your partner, and tap early and often. Never slam, never crank a submission, and always respect a tap. Your partner's safety is your job.

    Competition

    Test yourself when you're ready

    We take teams to regional IBJJF-style and submission-only tournaments around New England every semester, and the club has hosted its own Harvard Jiu-Jitsu Invitational. Competing is never required, and plenty of members never do. Those who want to get coaching, drilling partners, and a crowd of teammates in their corner.
